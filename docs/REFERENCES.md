# References and legal position

## Where circuit knowledge comes from

RF-Rig models circuit *families*. The knowledge behind each one is public and
comes from four kinds of source:

1. **Component-level analyses.** Several long-running electronics sites publish
   node-by-node analyses of classic effect circuits, with component values,
   measured transfer functions and explanations of each stage. ElectroSmash's
   analyses of the common overdrive, distortion, fuzz, phaser and chorus
   circuits are the best known.
2. **Community traces.** Builder forums (freestompboxes and similar) trace
   unpotted units and publish schematics; several famous circuits that shipped
   under epoxy were traced this way years ago.
3. **Patents.** Effect circuits were patented, those patents expired decades
   ago, and a patent's whole purpose is public disclosure of how the thing
   works.
4. **Datasheets.** Diodes (1N4148, 1N34A), transconductance amplifiers
   (CA3080/LM13700), companders (NE570) and bucket-brigade devices (MN3007,
   MN3005) are documented by their manufacturers, including the parameters this
   project's equations use directly.

Anything measured on a real unit belongs alongside these, and the procedure for
doing that is in [`MEASUREMENT.md`](MEASUREMENT.md).

## Method literature

The techniques, not the circuits:

* Yeh, Abel and Smith — *Automated Physical Modeling of Nonlinear Audio
  Circuits for Real-Time Audio Effects* (the nodal DK method).
* Werner — wave digital filters with nonlinearities, on which most single-diode
  stompbox models are built.
* Parker, Zavalishin and Le Bivic — *Reducing the Aliasing of Nonlinear
  Waveshaping Using Continuous-Time Convolution* (antiderivative antialiasing).
* Valimaki and Huovilainen on oversampling and polyphase half-band structures
  for virtual analog.
* Schroeder, Jot and Dattorro on all-pass diffusion and feedback delay networks,
  which is what the plate mode is.

## What this project will not do

* **No firmware.** Digital effects contain copyrighted code. If a digital pedal
  is ever modelled here, the user supplies the dump through RackForge's
  `[[resources]]` mechanism — the same arrangement RF-J1080V and the SC-VA
  plugin already use — and the package never carries it.
* **No trademarks, no brand names, no product names.** Not in the code, not in
  the interface, not in the documentation. Each pedal is named for what it does
  and described by its circuit family.
* **No artwork or photographs.** The branding is generated by
  `tools/generate-branding.py`, which is committed so the visual identity is
  reviewable and provably this project's own.
* **No claim of association.** RF-Rig is not affiliated with or endorsed by any
  manufacturer.

Circuit topologies are not copyrightable subject matter, and every patent
involved has long expired. What is protected — brand, artwork, firmware,
product identity — is exactly what this project stays away from. That position
is stated in [`../NOTICE.md`](../NOTICE.md) and is meant to survive contact with
a lawyer, not just with a forum argument.
